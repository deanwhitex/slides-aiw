What every expert agrees on

Seven laws

  • 01You are selling the meeting, not the website.
  • 02How you say it is 85%+ of the result. Tone beats words.
  • 03Earn their interest in 30 seconds, then ask.
  • 04Give them an out. Lower the stakes.
  • 05It is a numbers game. Volume beats luck.
  • 06Build the list and script up front. Don't research mid-dial.
  • 07Always ask for the meeting. No ask means a 0% chance.
The one rule

Sell the meeting.
Not the website.

You are earning fifteen minutes, not closing a deal on the phone. Get them on a proper call, then the close has room to happen. "If you don't ask for the meeting, you have a 0% chance of booking it."

The whole call has one job: book the next call.
The biggest lever

Tone is the job

"It's not what you say. It's how you say it."

Delivery is 85 to 95% of the result. The same words book a call or get a hang-up, depending on tone, pace and confidence. A good tone makes an average script work. A bad tone wastes a great one.

The structure

Every call, five moves

1 Open› 2 Value› 3 Ask› 4 Objection› 5 Book

Open: earn the first few seconds. Value: who you are, why them, what you want. Ask: go for a specific time. Objection: handle it. Book: lock the time and number.

Pick your style

Two styles that both win

Style A · Take control

Value first

You talk first, deliver value, then ask for the meeting. Fast and direct. Good for owners who want you to get to the point.

Style B · Disarm first

Permission first

Permission, curiosity, a short riff, then the ask. Lower pressure. Good for a cold local owner who isn't expecting you.

Calling local owners? Blend them. Open with permission and curiosity, then run the value statement and the revenue hook.

Opener · take control

The assumptive formality

"Hey [name], this is [you] from [company]. How are you."

Say "how are you" with a downward inflection, like a statement, not a question. It gets a quick formality back and they forget it's a cold call. An upward inflection invites them to stall. A downward one keeps control.

Opener · disarm first

Permission and the out

"Hey John... oh, hey John, it's [you] over at [company]. I know I'm catching you out of the blue, but do you mind if I grab half a minute? I'll tell you why I called and you tell me if it's relevant."

  • 01Double-tap the name. The "ums" are on purpose, they sound human.
  • 02Then a softball: "Heard of us, by any chance?" Not a qualifying question.
  • 03Give the out: "stop me any time." It keeps them comfortable.
Opener · what kills it

Don't open like this

  • 01"Am I speaking with the owner?" then a pause. Triggers their guard.
  • 02"Did I catch you at a bad time?" Almost everyone says yes. You set the trap.
  • 03"You're going to hate me, this is a cold call..." too many ways to lose it.
  • 04Leading with questions before you've said why you called.
The value statement

Who, why, what in 30 seconds

W Who you are› W Why them› W What you want

The burden is on you to be worth their time before you ask for anything. Keep the skeleton the same, swap the niche and the pain per prospect. The "what you want" is always the same: time on their calendar.

The value statement · template

Plug and play

Swap only the gold parts

"I'm reaching out because I work with [your niche] on getting found online and turning their website into a booking machine, usually by [how you do it]. I called out of the blue, so I'm really just looking to grab 15 minutes this week. How's your calendar Wednesday or Thursday?"

Small changes per prospect. Big difference at scale.
Disarm first · the riff

Build curiosity, not features

  • 01A quick line on who you are.
  • 02Name the problem everyone in their seat lives with.
  • 03Add a little skepticism. Don't be all sunshine like every other rep.
  • 04Land your one strong point with a vivid, simple picture.

Don't tickle their interest, blow their mind in under thirty seconds. Show them you understand their problem better than they do.

Our hook · from the KCA playbook

Show the money on the table

"It's fair to assume you're doing around [X] a month, right?"

Use their own numbers. Roughly how many jobs a month, times a typical job. State your guess, then anchor the gap: if the site brought in even a couple more a month, that's [Z] a month from the same business, without spending more on ads. Their maths sells the call, not you.

The ask

Assume the yes

Not "if you're interested, would you maybe...". Say: "I'm looking to grab 15 minutes this week. How's Thursday or Friday?" The meeting is implied, not requested. Offer two specific times, never "whenever suits you".

Objections

It's never a real no at first

People aren't wired to give their time away. Most owners say no before they've heard a thing. Stay calm, and remember what you're actually selling: not the website, just 15 minutes. So every objection is simply another chance to ask.

Don't argue. Add a reason. Ask again.
Objections · what you'll actually hear

The top five

  • 01"I already have a website." Most do. It rarely brings them work.
  • 02"I already have a guy." Good. Offer a second opinion, not a fight.
  • 03"Not interested." A reflex. They haven't heard the one thing yet.
  • 04"I'm with a customer right now." Fair. Get the number, not the no.
  • 05"Just send me some info." A polite brush-off. Book first, send after.
One move handles all of them. Here's the ladder.
Objections · the flow

One move, every time

  • 01You ask for a specific time.
  • 02First no? Acknowledge, reassert, advance. Then ask again.
  • 03They push again? Ask, clarify, expand. Then ask again.
  • 04Still no? Get their number, or bow out clean.

Same shape on every rung: don't argue, reassert the value, end on a question that goes back to the time. An objection is never a wall, it's just the next rung.

Objections · the first no

Acknowledge. Reassert. Advance.

They say: "I already have a website"
  • 01Acknowledge. "Most guys I call already have one."
  • 02Reassert. "That's the point. Plenty look fine but never bring a job. Worth 15 minutes to see if yours is one."
  • 03Advance. "How's Thursday or Friday?"

Don't argue, and don't sell the switch on the phone. Acknowledge it, reassert the value, ask again.

Objections · they push again

Ask. Clarify. Expand.

They say: "It's working fine"
  • 01Ask. "When's the last time your site actually got you a call?"
  • 02Clarify. "And the reason I ask is, most owners can't remember."
  • 03Expand. "That's usually the gap, and exactly what the 15 minutes is for. Thursday or Friday?"

Never ask a bare question. Always attach "and the reason I ask is". And always end on a question that goes back to the time, never on a statement.

Objections · when it's a real no

Get the number, or bow out

  • 01"I'm with a customer" isn't a no. "What's your number? I'll text you and we'll grab a time." The number is the win.
  • 02"Just send me info" is a brush-off. Book the 15 minutes first, then send.
  • 03A real dead end? Exit clean: "Let me update our records so we stop bugging you."
  • 04A no isn't personal. It's a numbers game. Make the next dial.
Book it

Lock it down

  • 01Offer a specific time. Never "when suits you?".
  • 02Get the number, repeat it back.
  • 03Lower the stakes: a quick 15-minute intro, not a big commitment.
  • 04Text within five minutes. Never just a cold calendar link.
  • 05Qualify lightly after the yes. It protects your show rate.
Delivery · how to sound

Low and slow, assumptive

  • 01Downward inflection. Statements, not questions.
  • 02Low and slow, just above monotone. The calm "late-night DJ" voice.
  • 03Natural pauses and a few "ums" so you don't sound like you're reading.
  • 04Assumptive words: "I'm looking to grab 15 minutes", not "if you're interested".
  • 05Drop the "I'm bothering them" belief. It leaks into your tone.
Before you dial

Coil the spring

  • 01Build the list and script before you start. Zero minutes to begin.
  • 02Keep lists tight: same niche, same area, so one script fits all.
  • 03You only need a name, a business, and a number. No deep research per call.
  • 04Call mornings and late afternoon. Use direct or mobile numbers.
  • 05Rotate numbers on a real campaign. Don't burn your own mobile.
Gotchas

Don't do this

  • 01Don't sell the website or run discovery on the cold call.
  • 02Don't accept "send me info" without asking for the meeting first.
  • 03Don't argue "I already have a guy". Double down on the time.
  • 04Don't research between every dial, or make too few dials.
  • 05Don't freestyle early. Run the script until it's muscle memory.
Best practices

Do this every time

  • 01Lead with value, ask for the meeting, offer a specific day.
  • 02On an objection: reassert and ask again. Attach "the reason I ask" to any question.
  • 03Rehearse ten minutes a day. Review your call recordings.
  • 04Track dials, answer rate, set rate. Fix the one that's low.
  • 05Carry what you learn on a lost call into the next one.
Now we drill

Role-play, live

  • 01Pair up. One caller, one local business owner.
  • 02Open, value statement, revenue hook, go for the time.
  • 03Owner throws an objection. Work the ladder: reassert, then ask again.
  • 04Swap. Then a few on the hot seat.
Remember

Book the call

Value first. Their numbers. Assume the yes. Don't argue the objection, double down on it. Lock the time. Follow up fast. Then do it again.

Same framework. Your niche. Run it.
